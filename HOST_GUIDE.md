# Host guide — The GIF Break

## The format

Use one host laptop and a TV/projector or screen share. Suggested grouping: teams of 3–5, with 2–8 teams in the page. Each team gets one printed answer sheet and a pen. Players watch and discuss; the host controls the page. Nobody needs to log in or install an app. This is shared-screen play, not synchronized phone multiplayer.

There are 100 questions in ten rounds of ten. Play at the room’s pace: if teams answer quickly, reveal and score immediately. The timer is optional; its default is 45 seconds and the host starts it manually. Offer a hint to everyone when needed, without a scoring penalty. There is no fixed session duration.

## Read these rules aloud

“You will see 100 short clips in ten rounds. Write the movie, series or source, not just the actor. One correct answer earns one point. Spelling and equivalent translated titles are fine. A film franchise is enough when the answer key allows it; you do not need a year or a sequel number. Rounds four and ten ask for TV series, not just character names. Question 30 is a video-game wildcard. No searching. We will show a hint to everyone when needed. The host's answer key settles ambiguity.”

Films such as Harry Potter, Pirates of the Caribbean, Toy Story and Rocky allow the broad franchise answer. Finding Nemo and Finding Dory are both accepted for q14 because the source tagging is ambiguous. q39 requires Wednesday/Mercredi; q40 requires The Mandalorian. q30 accepts Red Alert as well as the full title. Announce those category rules upfront rather than changing them after answers arrive.

## Pacing the ten rounds

Give out all three pages of the printable answer sheet to each team. Questions 1–40 retain the original order; questions 41–100 add classic movies, animation, comedy, heroes, more films and another TV round. Allow a short score check after each round and a break halfway through if the group wants one. Fast guesses can move straight to the reveal; the host decides when everyone is ready.

## Run a question

Load the clip. Once everyone can see it, optionally press Start timer. Offer the hint when appropriate. At zero, ask for pens down; the page does not reveal automatically. Press Reveal answer, then ask teams to raise their sheets or self-report. Toggle each correct team's button once: it awards exactly one point. Press Next.

The timer pauses when the tab is hidden. Hide motion removes the moving image and stops the countdown; Show motion reloads it. Revealed questions stay revealed when revisited. A new question resets to the chosen duration. Saved progress can be resumed after a refresh.

Keyboard: Space starts/pauses the timer, R reveals, H shows the hint, arrows navigate, M hides/shows motion. When a button has focus, Space retains the browser's native button action rather than also toggling the timer.

## Scoring and interruptions

Maximum 100 points. No speed bonus and no half-points: make it social, not argumentative. Score in the page after each answer; verify totals at round breaks. Correct mistaken awards by toggling them off. The page tracks each team/question combination, so revisiting does not double-award.

If a GIF fails, retry once. If it still fails or you discover a spoiler/incorrect source, void it for ALL teams. Its points are excluded and the maximum decreases equally. A shared win is simpler and fairer than an improvised factual tie-breaker.

Use Finish / scores to show the leaderboard, then export scores as a backup. Avoid Reset/New game until the event is done. Ask the room which clip they recognized fastest or which film they would rewatch to fill the final minutes.
